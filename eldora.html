Eldora

Bridge generations by strengthening community through advantageous connections and housing.

Tools Figma
Target group 1 18-26 years olds anticipating budget friendly housing
Target group 2 55-70 years olds in need for help with household tasks

Elevator pitch

“Eldora” - intergenerational platform, where one’s shortcoming could become a beneficial transaction for another. Designed to address two urgent urban challenges: housing insecurity and social isolation, the platform also offers practical support for older adults, creating mutually beneficial living arrangements. Unlike traditional matchmaking services, “Eldora” lets users manually choose compatible living partners through filters, turning spare rooms into shared homes built on trust and mutual benefit.

Seniors gain companionship, help and additional finances; young people save money while gaining stability. The platform earns through service fees and promoted listings, offering a scalable, community-based solution to the current housing landscape.

Mission

The mission is to create a digital product joining people of different stages of life and exploring benefits they can offer to each other regarding care and housing needs.

Value proposition

  • For young adults: affordable housing in exchange for performing household tasks.
  • For seniors: assistance with household tasks and companionship in exchange for discounted rent.

Research market and target audience

I chose to focus on the issue regarding “affordable housing”. By creating a digital platform, Eldora, I am addressing a dual challenge by targeting two distinct age demographics. The first group consists of seniors who may live in underutilized homes and need physical assistance and supplemental income. The second group includes young adults seeking affordable housing and financial stability.

Seniors, 55-70 years old

20%of older Europeans now live alone, a factor strongly linked to emotional and physical health decline (BMC Geriatrics, 2025)
47%of seniors in the EU occupy underutilized dwellings, compared to 31% of working-age adults (Eurostat, 2020)
65%of 360 seniors surveyed said loneliness and isolation had negatively impacted their health prior to moving into a community setting (Montgomery, 2025)

Young adults, 18-26 years old

21%of the EU population, 93.3 million people, were at risk of poverty or social exclusion in 2024 (Eurostat, 2024)
31%of Generation Z adults report living at home due to an inability to afford rent or home ownership (Credit Karma, 2024)
26%of young people (aged 15-29 years) in the EU lived in overcrowded dwellings, 9.2 percentage points more than the population overall (Eurostat, 2024)

Target group 1: Young adults (18-26 years old)

  • A need to budget
  • Able to do assigned tasks
  • Possible financial instability
  • No children of their own
  • Low-medium income
  • Tech-savvy

Target group 2: Seniors (55-70 years old)

  • Lives alone
  • Can provide a separate place for a tenant
  • Unsteady-decreasing health
  • Has at least minimal knowledge of technology
  • Ownership of under-occupied space
  • May struggle financially

The goal for young adults

To offer young adults a cost-effective housing alternative where they can reduce costs by assisting homeowners with household tasks.

The goal for seniors

To provide seniors with a thoughtful solution by connecting them with reliable tenants who can assist with daily, weekly or monthly household tasks.

Following steps

To research conditions each generation is forced to live with and the biggest challenges they face regarding living costs, housing management, mental wellbeing. To look into the city's population’s demographics based on location, the potential ways to comfortably accommodate and connect people. To look into income statistics and compare living costs.

Scenarios

John

John signs up on Eldora and finds Charles, a 65 year old retired librarian who has an extra room in his well-connected urban apartment.

  • Difficulty finding affordable housing near work.
  • A desire for independence but limited savings.
  • Anxiety about financial instability and debt.

Sofia

Sofia is a retired teacher living in a two bedroom house in a quiet suburban neighborhood. She connects with Anna, a 24-year-old graduate student. Anna offers to assist with grocery shopping and light cleaning in exchange for financially affordable rent.

  • Loneliness and lack of daily social interactions.
  • Limited mobility, making household chores harder.

Competitor analysis

Looking at the European market, there’s a trend of intergenerational platforms emerging, utilizing co-housing and rent’s reliance on pre-decided conditions. The usual target group is 70+ years old and older, vulnerable seniors.

The biggest competitor, “Homeshare”, enables two unrelated people to share a home and their lives for the mutual benefit, through an exchange of services: a homesharer provides support and help in the home, a householder provides free or low-cost accommodation. Another significant competitor, “TANDEMS”, is a platform specializing in intergeneration co-housing arrangements and works with a model of lowering monthly rent in exchange of hourly work.

Strategic differences

Matching system

The model “Eldora” operates on is digitally based, a system, where the matchmaking is mostly done by filtering. It’s important to give power for the user to pick the best match possible based on applied requirements.

Target demographics

The main demographic of the app is seniors, who have not yet reached the latest stages of life, have good cognitive functions and are not crumbling health wise. The concept is not focused on medical care, instead it works on help provision and companionship to the homeowners with estate space.

Urban focus

The app’s model also focuses on economic and social consequences of the current housing market, solving dual problem: under-dwelled young people and over-dwelled seniors, creating a balance, benefiting the users not only monetary wise, but improving urban infrastructure.

Product ecosystem

“Eldora” not only offers digital matchmaking, manually done by users, but provides interface with tasks management and ensures an easier and faster connection between tenant and the landlord.

Key interdependencies

Trust and safety

The success of the platform hinges on building trust between landlords and tenants. In order to avoid exploitation, it’s important to create mutual user dependency. Features like: verified profiles, task tracking, emergency option, confirmation features, requiring mutual agreement, ensure transparency.

Effective matching

The power of choosing the most beneficial offer is mainly in the user's hand. With the variety of precise filters, they can model their choices and then trust the system to bring it to them. However, there’s a section on the main page, where the user would be met with systematically suggested offers, picked by the app.

Active engagement

Regular engagement with both demographics via notifications, tasks engagement and reminders are critical to retain users. The tasks system requires daily/weekly tasks confirmation for the monthly accumulation. To keep track of the tasks, the users would have no choice but to rely on the app.

Scalability

The app must scale seamlessly as the user base grows, requiring efficient resource allocation and modular infrastructure.

Revenue model

“Eldora’s” primary revenue model revolves around a service fee mechanism, ensuring a straightforward and transparent pricing structure. The platform’s model charges a fixed fee or a percentage of the first month’s rent for each successful match facilitated by the platform. In addition to the core service fee, promoted listings offer a significant supplementary revenue stream.
